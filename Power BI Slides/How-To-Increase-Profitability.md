# Final Section: Can the Café Become More Profitable?

This final section of the dashboard focuses on evaluating whether the café business can improve its profitability. The analysis is structured across multiple slides, using interactive navigation buttons and hidden pages to guide users through each stage of the evaluation.

---

# Slide 1: Fixed vs Variable Costs Analysis

## Objective

To understand how cost structure affects profitability.

### Visuals Used:

* Waterfall chart (for fixed costs by month)
* Waterfall chart (for variable costs by month)
* Line graph comparing revenue and variable costs
* Navigation buttons to hidden slides

I used hidden slides and interactive buttons to allow users to switch between Fixed Cost and Variable Cost views.

---

## Fixed Costs

<img width="1422" height="796" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9d3a6c2c-5ba4-449e-9517-3aac837a080e" />

Fixed costs remain constant regardless of sales volume.
Examples include:

* Staff salaries
* Rent
* Utilities (base charges)

### Insight (Commentary Added on Slide):

Fixed costs do not decrease during holidays, even when revenue drops. This creates pressure on profitability during low-sales months. Since these expenses must be paid regardless of performance, they significantly affect net profit during semester breaks.

## Variable Costs

<img width="1413" height="792" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/08e980d8-2c9c-4072-86d6-e7042ac996e7" />


Variable costs change based on sales volume.
Examples include:

* Ingredients
* Packaging
* Electricity usage from ovens and equipment

### Insight (Commentary Added on Slide):

Variable costs closely follow revenue trends. When sales increase, ingredient usage and operational expenses increase proportionally. This confirms that higher sales drive higher variable expenses, but they also generate higher revenue.

The waterfall charts clearly show cost accumulation over time, while the line graph demonstrates the direct relationship between revenue and variable costs.

# Final Slide: Recommendations Page

<img width="1411" height="796" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c181c605-783e-41aa-872f-58d3562725e6" />

This page highlights key problems and provides actionable solutions to improve profitability. Hidden slides were used again, with navigation buttons allowing users to explore each recommendation in detail.

---

## Problem 1: Losses During Holidays

<img width="1422" height="794" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4501e8c0-63b2-4de5-9bd4-6974b2bb51ce" />

### Issue:

During semester breaks, total costs exceed revenue, leading to net losses.

### Solution:

* Reduce staff numbers during low-demand periods.
* Adjust operating hours (open later, close earlier).
* Minimize utility usage during off-peak periods.

This aligns staffing and operations with demand, reducing unnecessary fixed and variable expenses.

---

## Problem 2: Not Meeting Sales Target

<img width="1410" height="783" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3b6f977a-6907-49b2-9028-bb2975710374" />


### Issue:

The café did not achieve the $500,000 sales target in any semester.

### Solution:

* Introduce promotions and weekly themed events.
* Offer coupons and loyalty rewards.
* Expand delivery services across locations.
* Host competitions and engagement activities.

These strategies aim to increase sales volume and attract more customers, especially during slower periods.

---

## Problem 3: High Sales but Lower Margins on Some Items

<img width="1416" height="799" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a763bc4d-34d8-4ae3-ae74-709eee049eec" />

### Issue:

Some high-demand items generate strong sales but relatively lower profit margins.

### Solution:

* Adjust pricing slightly for high-demand items.
* Introduce peak-hour pricing strategies.
* Focus marketing on high-margin products.
* Optimize supplier sourcing to reduce ingredient costs.

This ensures popular items contribute more effectively to overall profitability.
